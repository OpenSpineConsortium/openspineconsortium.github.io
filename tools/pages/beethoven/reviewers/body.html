Beethoven

For reviewers and security offices

Beethoven is a browser extension that starts, watches and stops a Claude Code worker in your own account on your university's cluster. When the worker connects, it hands you the link that opens that session at claude.ai and in the Claude app. It reaches the cluster one way: through the university's Open OnDemand portal, in your own browser session, behind the university's VPN (GlobalProtect at Wayne State). It holds no site permission on claude.ai and never asks for one, never sees your Claude sign-in or your cluster password, installs nothing outside the browser, and runs no code it did not ship with.

What it never does

  • Beethoven has no site permission on claude.ai or any Anthropic page and never asks for one. It does not run on those sites, does not read them and does not change them. It opens an Anthropic page in a new tab only as a bookmark would (the sign-in page Claude Code printed during a sitting you started, your session's claude.ai/code link, the connectors settings page for the optional Google Drive step, the plans page); nothing of the extension runs there.
  • Beethoven never sees your Claude sign-in. You sign in to Claude Code yourself, on Anthropic's own page; the one-time code you paste into the extension's box is typed once into your own job's terminal on your cluster and is not stored, not logged and not sent anywhere else.
  • Beethoven never sees your cluster password or verification code. You type them into your browser's own sign-in box or the portal's own pages, which it never reads; it never signs in for you and never touches a multi-factor step. Your browser's password manager may remember the password, as it would for any website; Beethoven never reads, stores or fills it.
  • Beethoven installs nothing outside the browser: no helper, no native program, no installer. Versions 12.0.0 to 15.0.0 had a direct way over ssh through such a helper; 16.0.0 removed it whole, with its permission.
  • Beethoven runs no code it did not ship with. Its daily update check fetches a signed list, not a program.
  • Beethoven asks for a website only from a click, one at a time: your university's Open OnDemand portal. It refuses an address on claude.ai, claude.com or anthropic.com before asking the browser for anything. To learn whether the portal is reachable, it sends the portal's address a request that carries no sign-in or cookie and learns only whether an answer came (every three seconds at most while it waits for the VPN with one of its pages open). Its only other requests go to the maintainer: the approval check (the account name your portal showed, your portal's address, the product name and its version, and the installation identifier, a random number made when its agreement is accepted, not derived from the device), sent only after the portal accepted your sign-in; and a daily fetch of the service's address and the signed list of withdrawn versions, which carries nothing about you.
  • Beethoven sends its maintainer no usage data, no error reports and no activation request; the approval check is the only thing it sends about you. Its audit log never contains what you typed to sign in and is never transmitted.

Permissions and code

Permissions: storage, alarms, scripting and sidePanel. One optional host permission, https://*/*, which the extension asks your browser to grant for the one portal website, only inside a click (Agree and continue on its welcome page, Allow again in its panel, Change in its Settings); no host permission at install time, and none on any Anthropic domain: it refuses an address on claude.ai, claude.com or anthropic.com before asking for anything. Never requested: activeTab, cookies, tabs, webRequest, webRequestBlocking, declarativeNetRequest, debugger, identity, nativeMessaging (requested from 12.0.0 to 15.0.0 for the direct way's helper, removed with it in 16.0.0), clipboardRead.

scripting loads the extension's page script only into a tab it opened itself on the granted portal: where the portal does not share its sign-in with the extension's own requests, and on the portal's terminal page for its one-time key step, where it reads only the page's title and types one line. No page script is registered.

No remote code: the daily update check fetches one signed list (killlist.json) and checks its signature against a key built into the extension; the list can withdraw a version and nothing else. No licence key: the one request that carries anything about the person is the approval check, which sends the account name the portal showed, the portal's hostname, the product, the version and the installation identifier to the maintainer's access service (beethoven-access.openspineconsortium.workers.dev, named in services.json) and is answered yes or no. The bundle is minified for size and not obfuscated. Security reports go to the notice address on the support page.

The one way: the portal behind the VPN

The extension reaches the cluster only through the university's Open OnDemand portal, from its own background where the browser shares the portal's sign-in with it, and otherwise through a portal tab it opened itself. When the portal does not answer (the VPN is off), the panel shows the VPN step with a link to the university's own page about it and keeps the student's press; it goes ahead by itself once the portal answers. When the portal asks for a sign-in, the extension opens the portal in a tab, in front, for the student to sign in in the browser's own box, and closes that tab once the student is signed in and has moved on (at most fifteen seconds later), so the browser can offer to save the password. A browser extension cannot open or connect the university's VPN, and this one does not try.

The commands it gives the cluster are a small fixed vocabulary of the lab's shared copy of the cluster-side tool, typed into the portal's own shell, and the lines the cluster prints are parsed as text and never executed. The only key involved is made on the cluster, by the student's one-time step in the portal's terminal, so that the portal's own shell stops asking for the password; nothing of it is in the browser.

Distribution and installations

Beethoven is published only as a private listing in the Chrome Web Store: the listing opens for the Google accounts in the lab's group of testers, which the maintainer adds one by one after approving a request. There is no public listing, no other store and no download from this site, and the package holds no program besides the extension itself. The access service ties each approved account to one installation, identified by the random installation identifier the extension makes when its agreement is accepted and keeps in its own storage (it is not derived from the device, and a reinstall makes a new one). A check from a second installation of the same account is refused, and the maintainer is told; the maintainer can move the approval or withdraw it. The service keeps the identifier and when it was first and last seen; of refused checks it keeps a count, the time of the last one and the first eight characters of a one-way hash of the refused installation's identifier. This is an access rule the extension and the service keep between them, not copy protection: it makes casual sharing fail, and the agreement makes sharing a breach.

Beethoven's use of information received from your browser complies with the Chrome Web Store User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements.
