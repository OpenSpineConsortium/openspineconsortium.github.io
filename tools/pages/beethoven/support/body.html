Beethoven: support and notices

Getting help

Start with the panel: open More (the ⋯ button), then Details for your lab lead. Run the checks names the piece that is not working and says in one sentence what to do about it. When you write for help, send the one line the panel shows, the list of checks as it stands (a screenshot is fine) and, if you like, the audit log exported from Settings; it holds one line per request the extension made to your portal and never what you typed: no password, no code and no key.

Students: the Trial Edition is free for approved institutional accounts (ask for access); your lab lead is the first line of support. Beyond that, the maintainer may help through the address below, at his discretion and without a response-time promise (agreement, section 9(b)).

How it works

Beethoven reaches the Wayne State grid in one of two ways, and tries the first one first:

  • The direct way, with no VPN: Beethoven talks to the grid's login node over ssh, through a small helper on your computer that comes inside Beethoven.
  • GlobalProtect, as in earlier versions: Beethoven works through the grid's Open OnDemand website, which needs the VPN.

After installing the extension (and once your account is approved):

  1. Accept the agreement on the welcome page that opens: tick the box, then Agree and continue. Show terms opens the full text, which you can save and print.
  2. Install the helper, once. The panel says One small download, once and offers the file for your computer. It is a short script inside Beethoven: nothing comes from this website. Save it, open it, and answer your computer's warning (below). Or choose Use GlobalProtect instead.
  3. Sign in to the grid, once. In Beethoven's own sign-in sheet, type your AccessID, then your password and the grid's code from Google Authenticator (not Microsoft Authenticator, which Wayne State uses for everything else) when the grid asks for them. Beethoven sends them only to the grid, inside the encrypted connection, and never keeps them. It then says This computer is set up.: from now on it reaches the grid directly, with no VPN. It asks for your password and code again only when Claude on the grid needs signing in again, or after you sign in from another computer.
  4. Sign in to Claude once, when the panel asks. A Claude tab opens; sign in with your own Claude account, approve, and paste the code it shows into the panel. The code is typed once into your own job's terminal on the grid and is not kept.
  5. Open my session. Your worker runs until you press Stop.

The worker is a job on the grid, not a program on your computer. Once the panel shows Open my session, close the panel, the browser or the laptop as you like: open claude.ai/code or Code in the Claude app from any device, pick the environment named after your grid, and carry on. The job queues its own successor before its time is up, and the successor continues the same session.

What the worker makes (figures, tables, files) shows in that claude.ai session. If you want results in Google Drive, Docs or Sheets, turn on Claude's Google connectors yourself in claude.ai's settings; Beethoven does not do it for you.

Installing

Beethoven is for Chrome, on a private listing in the Chrome Web Store that opens only for invited Google accounts. After the lab lead approves your request, an invitation comes to the Google account you gave on it. Open it in Chrome, signed in to that account, and install Beethoven from the listing like any other extension: no developer mode and no file to unpack. There is no other place to get it.

One approved account, one installation. The first time Beethoven checks your approval, that installation becomes the one your account is tied to. It is identified by a random number Beethoven makes when you accept its agreement, not by anything about your computer. A second computer, a second Chrome profile or a reinstall is a new installation: the panel says your account is set up on another computer, and your lab lead can move the approval to the new one. Beethoven works only for the AccessID that was approved.

The helper

A browser extension cannot open an ssh connection by itself, so the direct way needs a small helper on your computer. It comes inside Beethoven, so nothing is downloaded from a website; you install it once per computer, with no administrator rights. It connects only to the grid's ssh port (grid.wayne.edu, port 22), carries Beethoven's encrypted connection there and back, never sees your password or your code, holds no key, keeps no log of what it carries and runs nothing else. It is a short script that your system already knows how to run (Windows PowerShell on Windows, Perl on a Mac and on Linux), and it is not signed by a company, so your computer warns you the first time. The download is a small zip file:

  • Windows: right-click the zip, choose Extract All, then double-click install.cmd in the folder that opens. Windows warns you: on Open File - Security Warning ("Unknown Publisher") click Run; on Windows protected your PC click More info, then Run anyway. If Smart App Control blocks it with no way to run it, right-click the zip, choose Properties, tick Unblock, click OK, and extract it again. If your organization's security software stops it, use GlobalProtect instead.
  • Mac: double-click the zip, then open install.command in the folder that appears. On macOS 15 or later, click Done when the Mac stops it, then open System Settings, Privacy & Security, and click Open Anyway beside the blocked file; on macOS 14 or earlier, Control-click the file, choose Open, then Open again. Or, on any macOS, type bash and a space in Terminal, drag install.command in, and press Return, which usually avoids the warning. Nothing about the helper has been tried on a Mac yet.
  • Linux: unzip it, then run bash install.sh in the folder it made. Chrome installed as a Snap or a Flatpak cannot start the helper; use GlobalProtect there.

The installer asks you to press Enter (or Return), then says the helper is installed. Go back to Beethoven: it finds the helper by itself, or press Check again. The zip is made out to the Beethoven you saved it from, so use the one your own panel offers. To remove the helper, run the uninstaller the installer put beside it. A Chromebook cannot run the helper; use GlobalProtect.

Your grid code

The grid asks for a Google Authenticator code whenever you sign in with your password. When it does not accept one, its message names two causes, so Beethoven asks you which: Type the code again if you have a grid entry in Google Authenticator, or I don't have a grid code yet. Only then does it show Set up your grid code first, with two ways to set it up with the grid's own 2FA Setup app, which needs GlobalProtect once (approved in Microsoft Authenticator): Use this computer, or Use my phone for when GlobalProtect won't install on your computer. The phone way shows square codes for the GlobalProtect and Google Authenticator apps and for Wayne State's own instructions, each with its address printed under it. In 2FA Setup, press Launch, and when the job has started, Launch 2FA Setup: a new tab shows the square code to scan.

Already have a grid code? Don't run 2FA Setup: it replaces your code, and the old one stops working. The grid's own page says: "NOTICE: Running this app will destroy any existing Google Authenticator credentials already in place."

Wayne State's guides: Google Authenticator for the grid (KB 20242) and GlobalProtect (KB 20314). Beethoven never sees your code's secret, and never makes a code itself.

The key on your computer

After your first direct sign-in, Beethoven keeps a key of its own in this browser, encrypted under a second key the browser keeps beside it, and your grid account gets one line that lets that key do only a short list of things: start, watch and stop your worker. It cannot open a terminal, read your other files or sign a worker in to Claude. The key never leaves your browser; someone who could use this browser, or copy its files, could use it within that list.

  • Windows Hello or Touch ID is optional, and off unless you turn it on. Turned on (on the sign-in sheet, when Beethoven offers it once, or in Settings, Connection, Protect my key with Windows Hello / Touch ID), Beethoven asks for your fingerprint, face or PIN once each time you open the browser. Turn it off again in the same place. Ticked on the sign-in sheet, before the key exists, it protects the key from the start; turned on later, an older unprotected copy can stay in the browser's files for a while.
  • Forget this computer, in Settings, Connection, removes the key from Beethoven and its line from your grid account (a protected key is unlocked first, so the grid is told). Do it before you leave a computer or remove Beethoven. If the grid can't be reached, the line stays until you remove it from a terminal on the grid with hpcworker browser-key remove (your lab lead can help). The line is what lets a key in: once it is gone, a copy of the key is of no use.

When GlobalProtect is the only way

Beethoven works through GlobalProtect, as before, when there is no helper for your computer, when your computer won't run it, when your network blocks the grid's ssh port (Can't reach the grid directly; some hospital, hotel and public networks do), or when you choose GlobalProtect only in Settings, Connection. Choose Use GlobalProtect instead, turn on GlobalProtect and connect to protect.wayne.edu (approve in Microsoft Authenticator), press Continue, and sign in to the grid in the box Chrome shows; Beethoven never sees what you type there. Try the direct way again goes back.

If Beethoven says Stopped: the grid's identity didn't match, it sent nothing: no password, no code, no key. Tell your lab lead, and use GlobalProtect meanwhile.

Beethoven for VS Code

Start with the troubleshooter inside the extension: click Beethoven in VS Code's status bar, then Troubleshoot and repair. It names the broken piece and repairs what is safe; Show log has the details. The manual (search the command palette for Open the manual) has a table of symptoms. The same approval covers both editions, and the rest of this page (the notice address, disputes, security) applies to both.

Full Edition keys, if offered, are one-time term licenses and do not renew. Within 14 days of a paid purchase you may ask for a full refund for any reason, once; after that, no refund is due when the extension stops working because of an outside dependency named in section 7 of its agreement, and if such an outage lasts more than fourteen consecutive days during a paid term, the days are added to your license once it works again (Full Edition agreement, section 9). Your acceptance of an agreement is kept on your computer with the exact texts beside it; the privacy statement says where. Texts: Trial Edition License Agreement, Full Edition License Agreement, Privacy statement, Third-party notices.

Notice address

Notices under either agreement (section 19(a) notice of a dispute, Full Edition section 9(d) refund requests and 9(e) outage claims, privacy requests, requests for a paper copy) go to:

NOTICE ADDRESS TO BE PUBLISHED BY THE LICENSOR BEFORE ANY MARKETPLACE LISTING OR PAID KEY
(a postal address and an e-mail address that are not a university or hospital address)

Until then, notices go to the person who approved your account.

Disputes

Section 19 of the agreement: before any lawsuit, the party with a claim sends the other a signed written notice (name, the e-mail in the access record, the install id shown with your acceptance record on Beethoven's settings page, the problem, the outcome wanted) and both sides have 60 days to resolve it, with a call if either asks. Claims are individual (class-action waiver), heard by a judge (jury waiver) in Wayne County, Michigan or the Eastern District of Michigan, or in small-claims court where you live. There is no arbitration clause in this version. Nothing in the agreement takes away a right your state's law does not allow to be waived.

Refunds and outages

The Trial Edition is free, takes no payment and converts to nothing; approval can be withdrawn and the edition can end with 30 days' notice, and because nothing is paid nothing is refunded. When Beethoven stops working because of an outside dependency named in section 7 of the agreement (your university's cluster, portal, network or policies; Anthropic; your browser or the store you installed from; your own accounts), there is no claim against the licensor; access is restored where practical once the dependency works again (section 9(c)). A paid Full Edition, if ever offered, would be a separate agreement with its own terms.

Security

Report a security problem privately to the notice address above, with the version you use and how to reproduce the problem, and please do not publish it until it is fixed. Good-faith research within the law is welcome.

Your records

Your acceptance of the agreement (its version, the hashes of the texts, the summary and the sentence you ticked, the button's label, the time and the installation identifier) is kept in the extension's own storage in your browser with the exact texts beside it; Settings lets you save and print them. Licensor keeps access records for the life of your access and six years after.

Texts: Trial Edition License Agreement, Privacy statement.
