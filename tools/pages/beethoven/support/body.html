Beethoven: support and notices

Getting help

Start with the panel: open More (the ⋯ button), then Details for your lab lead. Run the checks names the piece that is not working and says in one sentence what to do about it. When you write for help, send the one line the panel shows, the list of checks as it stands (a screenshot is fine) and, if you like, the audit log exported from Settings; it holds one line per request the extension made to your grid and never what you typed: no password, no code and no key.

Students: the Trial Edition is free for approved institutional accounts (ask for access); your lab lead is the first line of support. Beyond that, the maintainer may help through the address below, at his discretion and without a response-time promise (agreement, section 9(b)).

How it works

Beethoven reaches the Wayne State grid one way: with GlobalProtect, Wayne State's VPN, on, through the grid's own website in your own Chrome. Nothing else is installed on your computer. When the grid needs your sign-in, Beethoven asks for your AccessID and password on its own sheet in the panel and sends them only to the grid; if you prefer, you sign in in the box Chrome itself shows instead (your grid sign-in).

After installing the extension (and once your account is approved):

  1. Accept the agreement on the welcome page that opens: tick the box, then Agree and continue. Show terms opens the full text, which you can save and print. Chrome then asks to let Beethoven reach the grid's website: choose Allow.
  2. Turn on GlobalProtect. Open GlobalProtect, connect to protect.wayne.edu and approve the sign-in in Microsoft Authenticator. The panel says Looking for the grid…, then Connected. A browser extension cannot open or connect GlobalProtect for you; Beethoven notices by itself, within a few seconds, when it is on.
  3. Sign in to the grid. The panel shows Sign in to the grid: "Type your AccessID and password. Beethoven signs in to the grid for you, in the background." Type your AccessID (not your e-mail address) and your password, leave Remember it on this computer unticked (below), and press Sign in. No tab opens; the panel says Signed in as your AccessID. If the grid says the password is wrong, Beethoven stops at once and does not try it again until you type it again. Use Chrome's sign-in box instead is the other way: Beethoven brings the grid's own box to the front, you sign in there, and Beethoven never sees what you type.
  4. One more thing, once. The first time, the grid may ask for your password a second time, in its own terminal. The panel says so and opens it; type your password there yourself (Beethoven types no password there, not even the one you gave it), and the grid's code from Google Authenticator if it asks, click I'm in, and Beethoven types one line that stops it asking again.
  5. Where your work goes. Read it, tick the box, then Connect.
  6. Sign in to Claude once, when the panel asks. A Claude tab opens; sign in with your own Claude account, approve, and paste the code it shows into the panel. The code is typed once into your own job's terminal on the grid and is not kept.
  7. Open my session. Your worker runs until you press Stop.

The worker is a job on the grid, not a program on your computer. Once the panel shows Open my session, close the panel, the browser or the laptop as you like: open claude.ai/code or Code in the Claude app from any device, pick the environment named after your grid, and carry on. The job queues its own successor before its time is up, and the successor continues the same session.

What the worker makes (figures, tables, files) shows in that claude.ai session. If you want results in Google Drive, Docs or Sheets, turn on Claude's Google connectors yourself in claude.ai's settings; Beethoven does not do it for you.

Your grid sign-in

Where your password goes. What you type on Sign in to the grid goes only to the grid's website: Beethoven answers only the grid's request for a sign-in that its own background check receives, never one that a tab, a proxy or another website asks for (a tab gets Chrome's own box). It never sends your password to us and never writes it into a log or the audit log. Beethoven never fills in Wayne State's sign-in pages, never touches Microsoft Authenticator or your grid code, and never types your password into a terminal. The sign-in sheet does not replace GlobalProtect: you still need it.

One wrong try. When the grid turns a password down, Beethoven stops and sends nothing more by itself, saved or typed, until you type the password again; the panel says "That password didn't work, and Beethoven stopped. Check it before you try again: after a few wrong tries your university may lock your AccessID. A password changed in the last half hour may not work yet." Wayne State disables an AccessID after the fifth wrong password, and getting it back can take up to three business days (KB 20082). Beethoven itself sends a wrong password at most once; in detail: Chrome re-sends its cached copy once for each request already started with it before the first refusal comes back; Beethoven then sends at most one; when the answered request is abandoned before the grid replies, Chrome may re-send that copy once. How many of these the grid counts is not yet measured. If the panel says "Two tries haven't worked.", stop trying and ask the C&IT Help Desk; they can turn a disabled AccessID back on. If Beethoven can't tell whether a password it sent worked (Chrome closed before the grid replied, or a portal tab asked you to sign in or loaded a page while Beethoven's sign-in was on its way), it counts that try as neither right nor wrong, but it doesn't send a saved password again until a password you type on the sheet works, or you save one again.

Unticked, as the box always starts, Beethoven keeps the password in Chrome's memory for that one sign-in only and removes it once the sign-in has been tried, whether or not it worked, and at the latest two minutes after you pressed Sign in.

At Wayne State, type your password each time. Wayne State's Strong Password Standard says: "Never save passwords in web browsers or computer programs; instead, type them in each time you need them." Beethoven is a program in your browser, so at Wayne State leave Remember it on this computer unticked, and don't let Chrome save the password either when you use its box. The sheet says the same: "Off unless you tick it. Your university may ask you to type your password each time; if it does, leave this off."

Remember it on this computer. The box reads Remember it on this computer, unlocked with Windows Hello (on a Mac, Touch ID; elsewhere, your screen lock) and is never ticked for you. If you tick it where your university allows that, Windows Hello or Touch ID asks you, when you press Sign in, to make a passkey with your AccessID followed by (grid) as its user name, for example ab1234 (grid). Beethoven then keeps your password only in this Chrome profile on this computer, encrypted so that only that passkey can open it, and never sends it to us. If your computer can't lock a saved password that way, the panel says so, signs you in that once and stops offering the box; if you cancel the passkey, it says "Not saved, but signing you in this once."

Unlocking. Each time you open Chrome, the first time Beethoven needs the grid, the panel shows Unlock your grid sign-in: click Unlock with Windows Hello (or Touch ID), or Type it instead. Once it is unlocked, Beethoven signs in by itself whenever the grid asks, until you close Chrome, also while the panel is closed, so that a press you made or Keep my worker running can go ahead. Until you unlock it, the Grid line says Signed out with Unlock, and Beethoven sends no password to the grid. A saved password the grid turned down (after you changed it, for example) is not tried again, even after Chrome restarts: Settings says "Saved, but it didn't work last time. Forget it, or type the new one when Beethoven asks." If the password you type then works, Beethoven forgets the old saved one by itself, unless it can tell that it is the same password; then it keeps it and uses it again.

Where the passkey lives. The passkey is kept by Windows Hello, your Mac's Passwords app (iCloud Keychain) or Chrome's password manager, not by Beethoven; that store may keep it in your Google account or in iCloud and offer it on your other devices, but the saved password itself stays only in this Chrome profile on this computer. Someone who can use this Chrome profile while your sign-in is unlocked, or who can unlock your passkey, could sign in to the grid with it.

Forget. In Beethoven's Settings, under Your grid, Grid sign-in says "Saved on this computer for ab1234. It opens with Windows Hello." Click Forget and confirm Forget the saved sign-in?: Beethoven deletes the saved password and everything it keeps about it. Disconnect does the same, and so do changing the grid's address in Settings and uninstalling. Your list of passkeys may still show ab1234 (grid); you can delete it there, after Forget.

Two passkeys, told apart. The old passkey of Beethoven 12.0.0 to 15.0.0 is named with your AccessID alone, or beethoven, and can go (below). The one 19.0.0 uses for your grid sign-in is named <AccessID> (grid), for example ab1234 (grid); delete that one only after Forget.

Chrome stays signed in. Once the grid has accepted a password Beethoven gave it, Chrome keeps it in memory, as it keeps one you type into its own box, and uses it for the grid's pages you open yourself too, until you close Chrome or clear its browsing data with cookies; Forget and Disconnect don't end that.

Chrome's own box. Use Chrome's sign-in box instead, on the sheet, opens the grid's website in a tab and brings Chrome's own sign-in box to the front, as Beethoven 18.0.0 did; you sign in there, Beethoven never sees what you type, and the tab closes by itself once you are signed in and have moved on. Where Beethoven can't answer the grid's sign-in itself, this is the only way. Chrome may offer to save the password in its password manager; that is Chrome's, not Beethoven's: Beethoven never reads it, stores it or fills it in, and Wayne State's standard above applies to it the same way.

This sign-in came with Beethoven 19.0.0 and has been tried only in our own tests, against a stand-in for the grid and for a passkey, not yet on the real grid or with a real Windows Hello or Touch ID. If it does something this page does not say, open More, click Copy debug log and send it to your lab lead; it never holds your password.

Installing

Beethoven is for Chrome, on a private listing in the Chrome Web Store that opens only for invited Google accounts. After the lab lead approves your request, an invitation comes to the Google account you gave on it. Open it in Chrome, signed in to that account, and install Beethoven from the listing like any other extension: no developer mode and no file to unpack. There is no other place to get it.

One approved account, one installation. The first time Beethoven checks your approval, that installation becomes the one your account is tied to. It is identified by a random number Beethoven makes when you accept its agreement, not by anything about your computer. A second computer, a second Chrome profile or a reinstall is a new installation: the panel says your account is set up on another computer, and your lab lead can move the approval to the new one. Beethoven works only for the AccessID that was approved.

When Beethoven updates to 19.0.0, it asks you to accept its agreement once more, because the agreement now describes the grid sign-in.

When the grid is out of reach

GlobalProtect must be on each time you start, watch or stop your worker; your worker itself keeps running without it, and Open my session works without it. If you press Connect, Stop or anything else that needs the grid while GlobalProtect is off, Beethoven keeps your press and shows Turn on GlobalProtect. with How to set it up (Wayne State's own page). Connect GlobalProtect and wait: Beethoven carries on by itself as soon as the grid answers, and you need not press again. If the grid then needs your sign-in, the panel shows Sign in to the grid (or Unlock your grid sign-in, if you saved it). If Keep my worker running is on in Settings and Beethoven finds your worker ended without your Stop, it starts it again by itself, at most three times in a row.

Your grid code

The grid's own terminal, the one time Beethoven sends you there, may ask for a code from Google Authenticator (not Microsoft Authenticator, which Wayne State uses for everything else). Beethoven itself never asks for it, never sees its secret and never makes a code.

Already have a grid code? Don't run 2FA Setup: it replaces your code, and the old one stops working. The grid's own page says: "NOTICE: Running this app will destroy any existing Google Authenticator credentials already in place."

Wayne State's guides: Google Authenticator for the grid (KB 20242) and GlobalProtect (KB 20314).

What an earlier version may have left

Beethoven 12.0.0 to 15.0.0 also had a direct way to the grid, with a small helper on your computer, a sign-in sheet of its own and a key in your browser. It is gone: today's Beethoven has none of them. The sign-in sheet of 19.0.0 is a different thing: no helper, no key in your browser, no code, and GlobalProtect is still needed (your grid sign-in).

  • The key. Such a version added a key for this computer to your grid account. Today's Beethoven removes what it left in your browser, without reading the key, and Settings says once that the key is still in your grid account; Forget hides that note. Beethoven touches nothing on the grid for it: your lab lead can take the key off your account (or run hpcworker browser-key remove in a terminal on the grid).
  • The helper. Such a version also installed a small program, the Beethoven bridge. Today's Beethoven does not use it and cannot start it; you can leave it or remove it, and removing it changes nothing on the grid. On Windows, open Settings, then Apps (Installed apps on Windows 11, Apps & features on Windows 10), find Beethoven bridge and choose Uninstall. On a Mac, in the Finder choose Go, then Go to Folder, paste ~/Library/Application Support/Beethoven/bridge and double-click uninstall.command. On Linux, in a terminal: bash ~/.local/share/beethoven/bridge/uninstall.sh. A helper installed from a zip with Beethoven 14.0.0 or earlier is removed on Windows by uninstall.cmd in %LOCALAPPDATA%\Beethoven\bridge.
  • The old passkey. If you turned on Windows Hello or Touch ID protection then, that passkey is still in your system's or your browser's passkey list, named with your AccessID alone, or beethoven. Today's Beethoven never uses it, and it can go: delete it there. It is not the passkey 19.0.0 makes when you tick Remember it on this computer, which has your AccessID followed by (grid) as its user name (for example ab1234 (grid)); delete that one only after Forget.

Beethoven for VS Code

Start with the troubleshooter inside the extension: click Beethoven in VS Code's status bar, then Troubleshoot and repair. It names the broken piece and repairs what is safe; Show log has the details. The manual (search the command palette for Open the manual) has a table of symptoms. The same approval covers both editions, and the rest of this page (the notice address, disputes, security) applies to both.

Full Edition keys, if offered, are one-time term licenses and do not renew. Within 14 days of a paid purchase you may ask for a full refund for any reason, once; after that, no refund is due when the extension stops working because of an outside dependency named in section 7 of its agreement, and if such an outage lasts more than fourteen consecutive days during a paid term, the days are added to your license once it works again (Full Edition agreement, section 9). Your acceptance of an agreement is kept on your computer with the exact texts beside it; the privacy statement says where. Texts: Trial Edition License Agreement, Full Edition License Agreement, Privacy statement, Third-party notices.

Notice address

Notices under either agreement (section 19(a) notice of a dispute, Full Edition section 9(d) refund requests and 9(e) outage claims, privacy requests, requests for a paper copy) go to:

NOTICE ADDRESS TO BE PUBLISHED BY THE LICENSOR BEFORE ANY MARKETPLACE LISTING OR PAID KEY
(a postal address and an e-mail address that are not a university or hospital address)

Until then, notices go to the person who approved your account.

Disputes

Section 19 of the agreement: before any lawsuit, the party with a claim sends the other a signed written notice (name, the e-mail in the access record, the install id shown with your acceptance record on Beethoven's settings page, the problem, the outcome wanted) and both sides have 60 days to resolve it, with a call if either asks. Claims are individual (class-action waiver), heard by a judge (jury waiver) in Wayne County, Michigan or the Eastern District of Michigan, or in small-claims court where you live. There is no arbitration clause in this version. Nothing in the agreement takes away a right your state's law does not allow to be waived.

Refunds and outages

The Trial Edition is free, takes no payment and converts to nothing; approval can be withdrawn and the edition can end with 30 days' notice, and because nothing is paid nothing is refunded. When Beethoven stops working because of an outside dependency named in section 7 of the agreement (your university's cluster, portal, network or policies; Anthropic; your browser or the store you installed from; your own accounts), there is no claim against the licensor; access is restored where practical once the dependency works again (section 9(c)). A paid Full Edition, if ever offered, would be a separate agreement with its own terms.

Security

Report a security problem privately to the notice address above, with the version you use and how to reproduce the problem, and please do not publish it until it is fixed. Good-faith research within the law is welcome.

Your records

Your acceptance of the agreement (its version, the hashes of the texts, the summary and the sentence you ticked, the button's label, the time and the installation identifier) is kept in the extension's own storage in your browser with the exact texts beside it; Settings lets you save and print them. Licensor keeps access records for the life of your access and six years after.
