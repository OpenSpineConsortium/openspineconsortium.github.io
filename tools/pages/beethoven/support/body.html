Beethoven: support and notices

Getting help

Start with the panel: open More (the ⋯ button), then Details for your lab lead. Run the checks names the piece that is not working and says in one sentence what to do about it. When you write for help, send the one line the panel shows, the list of checks as it stands (a screenshot is fine) and, if you like, the audit log exported from Settings; it holds one line per request the extension made to your grid and never what you typed: no password, no code and no key.

Students: the Trial Edition is free for approved institutional accounts (ask for access); your lab lead is the first line of support. Beyond that, the maintainer may help through the address below, at his discretion and without a response-time promise (agreement, section 9(b)).

How it works

Beethoven reaches the Wayne State grid one way: with GlobalProtect, Wayne State's VPN, on, through the grid's own website in your own signed-in Chrome. Nothing else is installed on your computer, and Beethoven never asks for your grid password: you type it only into the sign-in box Chrome itself shows.

After installing the extension (and once your account is approved):

  1. Accept the agreement on the welcome page that opens: tick the box, then Agree and continue. Show terms opens the full text, which you can save and print. Chrome then asks to let Beethoven reach the grid's website: choose Allow.
  2. Turn on GlobalProtect. Open GlobalProtect, connect to protect.wayne.edu and approve the sign-in in Microsoft Authenticator. The panel says Looking for the grid…, then Connected. A browser extension cannot open or connect GlobalProtect for you; Beethoven notices by itself, within a few seconds, when it is on.
  3. Sign in to the grid in the box Chrome shows, with your AccessID and password. Beethoven brings that box to the front and never sees what you type there. Chrome can remember it for you (below).
  4. One more thing, once. The first time, the grid may ask for your password a second time, in its own terminal. The panel says so and opens it; type your password there (and the grid's code from Google Authenticator if it asks), click I'm in, and Beethoven types one line that stops it asking again.
  5. Where your work goes. Read it, tick the box, then Connect.
  6. Sign in to Claude once, when the panel asks. A Claude tab opens; sign in with your own Claude account, approve, and paste the code it shows into the panel. The code is typed once into your own job's terminal on the grid and is not kept.
  7. Open my session. Your worker runs until you press Stop.

The worker is a job on the grid, not a program on your computer. Once the panel shows Open my session, close the panel, the browser or the laptop as you like: open claude.ai/code or Code in the Claude app from any device, pick the environment named after your grid, and carry on. The job queues its own successor before its time is up, and the successor continues the same session.

What the worker makes (figures, tables, files) shows in that claude.ai session. If you want results in Google Drive, Docs or Sheets, turn on Claude's Google connectors yourself in claude.ai's settings; Beethoven does not do it for you.

Letting Chrome remember your grid sign-in

The grid's sign-in box is Chrome's own, so Chrome's password manager can remember your AccessID and password. When Chrome offers to save them after you sign in, choose Save; the next time the box appears, Chrome fills them in and you press Sign in. To have Chrome ask for Windows Hello (on a Mac, Touch ID or your Mac's password) before it fills them in, open Password settings under Your grid in Beethoven's Settings (or chrome://password-manager/settings) and turn on Use Windows Hello when filling passwords (on a Mac, Use your screen lock when filling passwords). The password stays in Chrome's password manager: Beethoven never reads it, stores it or fills it in.

Installing

Beethoven is for Chrome, on a private listing in the Chrome Web Store that opens only for invited Google accounts. After the lab lead approves your request, an invitation comes to the Google account you gave on it. Open it in Chrome, signed in to that account, and install Beethoven from the listing like any other extension: no developer mode and no file to unpack. There is no other place to get it.

One approved account, one installation. The first time Beethoven checks your approval, that installation becomes the one your account is tied to. It is identified by a random number Beethoven makes when you accept its agreement, not by anything about your computer. A second computer, a second Chrome profile or a reinstall is a new installation: the panel says your account is set up on another computer, and your lab lead can move the approval to the new one. Beethoven works only for the AccessID that was approved.

When the grid is out of reach

GlobalProtect must be on each time you start, watch or stop your worker; your worker itself keeps running without it, and Open my session works without it. If you press Connect, Stop or anything else that needs the grid while GlobalProtect is off, Beethoven keeps your press and shows Turn on GlobalProtect. with How to set it up (Wayne State's own page). Connect GlobalProtect and wait: Beethoven carries on by itself as soon as the grid answers, and you need not press again. If Keep my worker running is on in Settings and Beethoven finds your worker ended without your Stop, it starts it again by itself, at most three times in a row.

Your grid code

The grid's own terminal, the one time Beethoven sends you there, may ask for a code from Google Authenticator (not Microsoft Authenticator, which Wayne State uses for everything else). Beethoven itself never asks for it, never sees its secret and never makes a code.

Already have a grid code? Don't run 2FA Setup: it replaces your code, and the old one stops working. The grid's own page says: "NOTICE: Running this app will destroy any existing Google Authenticator credentials already in place."

Wayne State's guides: Google Authenticator for the grid (KB 20242) and GlobalProtect (KB 20314).

What an earlier version may have left

Beethoven 12.0.0 to 15.0.0 also had a direct way to the grid, with a small helper on your computer, a sign-in sheet of its own and a key in your browser. It is gone: today's Beethoven has none of them and never asks for your password or code on a page of its own.

  • The key. Such a version added a key for this computer to your grid account. Today's Beethoven removes what it left in your browser, without reading the key, and Settings says once that the key is still in your grid account; Forget hides that note. Beethoven touches nothing on the grid for it: your lab lead can take the key off your account (or run hpcworker browser-key remove in a terminal on the grid).
  • The helper. Such a version also installed a small program, the Beethoven bridge. Today's Beethoven does not use it and cannot start it; you can leave it or remove it, and removing it changes nothing on the grid. On Windows, open Settings, then Apps (Installed apps on Windows 11, Apps & features on Windows 10), find Beethoven bridge and choose Uninstall. On a Mac, in the Finder choose Go, then Go to Folder, paste ~/Library/Application Support/Beethoven/bridge and double-click uninstall.command. On Linux, in a terminal: bash ~/.local/share/beethoven/bridge/uninstall.sh. A helper installed from a zip with Beethoven 14.0.0 or earlier is removed on Windows by uninstall.cmd in %LOCALAPPDATA%\Beethoven\bridge.
  • A passkey. If you turned on Windows Hello or Touch ID protection then, the passkey named Beethoven is still in your system's or your browser's passkey list; delete it there.

Beethoven for VS Code

Start with the troubleshooter inside the extension: click Beethoven in VS Code's status bar, then Troubleshoot and repair. It names the broken piece and repairs what is safe; Show log has the details. The manual (search the command palette for Open the manual) has a table of symptoms. The same approval covers both editions, and the rest of this page (the notice address, disputes, security) applies to both.

Full Edition keys, if offered, are one-time term licenses and do not renew. Within 14 days of a paid purchase you may ask for a full refund for any reason, once; after that, no refund is due when the extension stops working because of an outside dependency named in section 7 of its agreement, and if such an outage lasts more than fourteen consecutive days during a paid term, the days are added to your license once it works again (Full Edition agreement, section 9). Your acceptance of an agreement is kept on your computer with the exact texts beside it; the privacy statement says where. Texts: Trial Edition License Agreement, Full Edition License Agreement, Privacy statement, Third-party notices.

Notice address

Notices under either agreement (section 19(a) notice of a dispute, Full Edition section 9(d) refund requests and 9(e) outage claims, privacy requests, requests for a paper copy) go to:

NOTICE ADDRESS TO BE PUBLISHED BY THE LICENSOR BEFORE ANY MARKETPLACE LISTING OR PAID KEY
(a postal address and an e-mail address that are not a university or hospital address)

Until then, notices go to the person who approved your account.

Disputes

Section 19 of the agreement: before any lawsuit, the party with a claim sends the other a signed written notice (name, the e-mail in the access record, the install id shown with your acceptance record on Beethoven's settings page, the problem, the outcome wanted) and both sides have 60 days to resolve it, with a call if either asks. Claims are individual (class-action waiver), heard by a judge (jury waiver) in Wayne County, Michigan or the Eastern District of Michigan, or in small-claims court where you live. There is no arbitration clause in this version. Nothing in the agreement takes away a right your state's law does not allow to be waived.

Refunds and outages

The Trial Edition is free, takes no payment and converts to nothing; approval can be withdrawn and the edition can end with 30 days' notice, and because nothing is paid nothing is refunded. When Beethoven stops working because of an outside dependency named in section 7 of the agreement (your university's cluster, portal, network or policies; Anthropic; your browser or the store you installed from; your own accounts), there is no claim against the licensor; access is restored where practical once the dependency works again (section 9(c)). A paid Full Edition, if ever offered, would be a separate agreement with its own terms.

Security

Report a security problem privately to the notice address above, with the version you use and how to reproduce the problem, and please do not publish it until it is fixed. Good-faith research within the law is welcome.

Your records

Your acceptance of the agreement (its version, the hashes of the texts, the summary and the sentence you ticked, the button's label, the time and the installation identifier) is kept in the extension's own storage in your browser with the exact texts beside it; Settings lets you save and print them. Licensor keeps access records for the life of your access and six years after.
